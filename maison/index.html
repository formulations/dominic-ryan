THE ETHOS

I've come to think of Maison as something more personal than a place to call home. It's a way of bringing together the things I've grown to appreciate, from the character of a room to the objects I've collected along the way.

Some of those interests have found their way into the spaces I've crafted. Others have become part of the symbols that I use to express myself. And while this philosophy may take different forms, I've always felt there's something that always connects them.

Now, here you'll find a little of what I've been revealing to you. And perhaps there's no better place to begin than the Aubremont Residence, where so much of my craft has found its expression.

There is a certain privilege in being entrusted with the character of a space. Through my personal contributions to the Aubremont Residence, I sought an interior that felt settled rather than staged — a place where furnishings, architectural details, and objects could sit comfortably alongside one another.

I paid particular attention to harmony: the placement of each piece, the way materials meet, and how light changes the character of the room throughout the day. Texture, tone, and a few carefully chosen details give the space its own personality and balance without asking too much of it.

What interests me most about interiors is often what takes time to notice. The film captures some of those quieter decisions — and the pleasure of seeing a room come to life.

—DOMINIC RYAN
THE SIGNATURE / 02

Two letters.
One point of view.

But the story of my Maison extends far beyond the spaces I've crafted.

The Aubremont Residence reflects choices I made, instincts I followed, and an understanding of beauty that has become my own over time. Yet those qualities aren’t confined to interiors. They follow me into the things I collect, the places I visit, and the ideas I choose to explore.

The DR monogram is perhaps the simplest expression of that connection. Not a representation of any one interest or discipline, but of the person to whom they all belong. Two letters, drawn from something more timeless than any particular creation: my name.

Perhaps I'll have more to share in time. Do take your time. For now, I shall leave you here.

Until we meet again.
